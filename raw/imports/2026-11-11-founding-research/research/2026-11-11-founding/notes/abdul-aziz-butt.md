# Abdul Aziz Butt / Bhat / Batt ("Abbaji"), said to be 1916–2016: public-record check

Scope: I tested each family claim against public sources. Family claims were not treated as evidence. No family memorial site was used. The spellings Butt, Bhat and Batt all appear in the sources for what is almost certainly one man. Other men with the same name are kept apart (see Q8).

Source key (used throughout):
- **[CL-1947]**: *Civil List of His Highness Government, Jammu and Kashmir* (Ranbir Government Press; printed 4-11-2003 Bikrami, about Jan–Feb 1947). Digital Library of India scan from the Allama Iqbal Library, University of Kashmir. https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.449300. **Primary government record.**
- **[CL-127]**: *Civil List of Jammu and Kashmir Government, No. 127, corrected up to 1st Katik 2009* (Bikrami, about Oct 1952; the Archive catalogue says 1954). https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481. **Primary government record.**
- **[CL-128]**: *Civil List … No. 128, corrected up to 1st Katik 2010* (about Oct 1953). https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.471671. Primary, but the OCR is scrambled for this entry.
- **[BHD]**: Bashir Bhadarwahi, *The History and Culture of Bhadarwah*, translated by Ta-Ha Mughal (Gulshan Books, Srinagar, 1st ed. 2023, ISBN 978-81-8339-419-2). It translates an Urdu original, *Bhaderwah ki Tareekh o Saqafat*, whose date I could not find. The biography "Professor Abdul Aziz Bhat" is in Section 4, pp. 774–775. https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah ; full text: https://archive.org/stream/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah/The%20History%20and%20Culture%20of%20Bhadarwah_djvu.txt. **Secondary local history.** It quotes his own writings ("in his own words", "Aziz Sahib writes"), so it is partly autobiographical.
- **[BHL]**: Sadaket Ali Malik, *Bhalessa: Cultural Heritage and History* (HSRA Publications, Bangalore, 1st ed. 2021, ISBN 978-93-90415-80-9), pp. 28–32. https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik ; full text: https://archive.org/stream/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik/B%20H%20A%20L%20E%20S%20S%20A%20by%20Sadaket%20Malik_djvu.txt. **Secondary local history.** It cites no sources, contradicts itself in places, and copies one sentence word for word from a 2017 KNS obituary (see Q7).
- **[GK-2021]**: Showkat Rashid Wani, "Lest we forget", *Greater Kashmir*, 17 Jan 2021. https://www.greaterkashmir.com/opinion/lest-we-forget-6/
- **[KNS-2017]**: "Eminent educationist Abdul Aziz Bhat passes away", KNS, posted 7 Apr 2017. https://www.knskashmir.com/Eminent-educationist-Abdul-Aziz-Bhat-passes-away-15731

All book and civil-list quotations come from Internet Archive OCR text. Where the OCR is damaged I quote only the legible parts and mark gaps with "…" or [brackets]. Bikrami-to-CE conversions are my own approximations. J&K records used the Bikrami era, which runs about 57 years ahead of CE for most of the year.

## Q1. Is there an independent public record of this man, and how reliable is it?

### Takeaway
Yes. Two J&K government Civil Lists (1947 and about 1952) record an "Abdul Aziz Butt" in the Kashmir Education Service with date of birth 26-7-1916. Two local histories (2021, 2023) give detailed biographies of "Professor Abdul Aziz Bhat/Batt" of Kilhotran, Bhalessa, born 26 July 1916, former Registrar of Jammu and Kashmir University. The records support a Bhalessa-born state educationist and University Registrar. They contradict or fail to support most of the university-faculty claims: founding faculty in 1948, Dean, professor of named subjects, PhD, Oxford/Warwick.

### Cited Findings
- [CL-127] lists, in section "XV.— Kashmir Education [Service]", Class III, the grade "… and Head Masters (200-15-350)", entry "19. Abdul Aziz Butt | 26-7-1916 | • | 23-2-1998 | 17-12-2001 … 19-1-2009". The column heads are "Date of birth", "Date of entry into Government service", "Date of promotion to present grade" and "Date of promotion to present pay". — [CL-127](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481)
- [CL-1947] lists, under "XV. Kashmir Education", Class [III], entry "34. Abdul Aziz Butt", the last name before "Category 1.—Inspector Technical Education". — [CL-1947](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.449300)
- [CL-128] (about 1953) still lists "17. Abdul Aziz Butt" in the same section. The OCR'd dates on that line belong to a neighbouring entry. — [CL-128](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.471671)
- [BHD] opens its biography: "Professor Abdul Aziz Bhat was born in the renowned Bhat family of village Kilhotran Tehsil Bhalesa, to Khwaja Sulla Bhat on 26 July 1916 AD." — [BHD](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- [BHL]: "Professor Abdul Aziz Batt. M.A, B.T, L.L.B, H.P., E.E.T&P (London) born on July 26, 1916 was one of the senior most educationist of J&K State" … "He served Registrar of the then Jammu and Kashmir University. Hailing from KilhotranBhalessa (Doda)". — [BHL](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- [GK-2021], a column by a University of Kashmir faculty member, says: "Professor Abdul Aziz served as the Registrar & Controller of the Jammu & Kashmir University, he was a great administrator." No surname or dates are given. — [GK-2021](https://www.greaterkashmir.com/opinion/lest-we-forget-6/)
- [BHL] separates "Abdul Aziz Batt Former Registrar" from "Abdul Aziz Batt of Kilhotran" and a third "Abdul Aziz Batt" in one list. Elsewhere it describes an "Abdul Aziz Batt, born in 1934 at Changa", a political figure and "Ex MLC". So the village had several men of this name. — [BHL](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)

### Inferences
- **The civil-list man and the book subject are very likely the same person.** All three sources give the same date of birth, 26-7-1916. The civil-list entry date 23-2-1998 Bikrami (about May–June 1941) matches [BHD]'s statement that he was appointed Second Teacher at Kishtwar High School "in the year 1941 AD". The civil lists are contemporaneous primary records, independent of the family and of the books. They are the strongest evidence found.
- **The books are weaker evidence.** Both are uncited local histories published 2021–2023, after his death (or near it). [BHD] draws partly on his own writings. [BHL] borrows text from an obituary. Where they agree with the civil lists the claim is well supported. Where they stand alone, treat them as single secondary sources.

## Q2. Birth, origins (Bhalessa / Bhaderwah / Srinagar) and the Ghulam Rasool Azad link

### Takeaway
Bhalessa birth (village Kilhotran) and the Bhaderwah ties are supported, and so is a later home in Srinagar (Gogjibagh). The birth date in the records is **26 July 1916**, not January 1916. Ghulam Rasool Azad, also born 1916 in Bhalessa, is named in the record as his childhood classmate.

### Cited Findings
- Date of birth "26-7-1916" for Abdul Aziz Butt, Kashmir Education Service. — [CL-127](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481)
- "…born in the renowned Bhat family of village Kilhotran Tehsil Bhalesa, to Khwaja Sulla Bhat on 26 July 1916 AD. He was a classmate of Ghulam Rasool Azad since childhood until the 10[th] Class". — [BHD, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- "born on July 26, 1916"; "Hailing from KilhotranBhalessa (Doda) he has proved to be a torch bearer of this area." — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- "Aziz lived in Gogjibagh area in Srinagar". — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- [BHD] lists "Jenab Abdul Aziz Butt former Registrar Jammu and Kashmir University, who is also the first M.A. of this place" among the Persian experts of the area. — [BHD, pp. 670–671](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- Ghulam Rasool Azad: "Ghulam Rasool Azad was born in the year 1916 to this peasant family." "There were only two schools then in Bhalessa - the Primary school Kilhotran and the Bhaderwah Amar Singh school." — [Roots of Bhalessa blog (Sadaket Malik), 4 Feb 2010](https://rootsofbhalessa.blogspot.com/2010/02/chapter-i-ii.html)
- [BHD] lists "Professor Umar-ul-Din Malik, Ghulam Rasool Azad, Khawaja Abdul Aziz Bhat, Kotwal Dina Nath Bhal, Khawaja Ghulam Nabi Azad and Professor Sultan Muhammad Khan" as noted people of the region "who have held high positions in the State Administration". — [BHD](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- Family details in [BHL]: "His father died in 1960 and mother four years later. He had three brothers: Nizamud Din, Gh Qadir Bhat, survived by three sons and three daughters. His son, Javid Ahmad, is first Muslim commercial pilot of Kashmir. Another son, Altaf Ahmad, retired as executive engineer and third son is a doctor." — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)

### Inferences
- The family's "January 1916" disagrees with three sources that all give 26 July. The civil list is a contemporaneous official record, so 26 July 1916 is the better-supported date. The family date could be a different reckoning or a recollection; I found no source for it.
- The children named in [BHL] (Javid Ahmad, a pilot; Altaf Ahmad, an engineer; a doctor son; three daughters) give the family a direct identity check. If they match, [BHL] is about the family's Abbaji.

### Gaps
- No source names his mother or gives a birth month other than July.
- The name "Abbaji" does not appear in any public source found.

## Q3. Schooling and degrees: Bhalessa school, Bhaderwah, GGM Science College, Aligarh LLB

### Takeaway
Primary schooling in Bhalessa and secondary schooling at Bhaderwah are supported. His degree college was **Prince of Wales College, Jammu (BA 1938)**, which was renamed Gandhi Memorial College in 1948, so the "GGM Science College" claim holds under the college's earlier name. "Bhaderwah Degree College" cannot have been where he studied: it opened in 1955, and [BHD] makes him its Principal in 1956. One secondary source supports an AMU LLB.

### Cited Findings
- Children sent to Kilhotran primary school with the young Ghulam Rasool Azad included "Ghulam Mohammed Batt, Ghulam Mohammed Kumhar, Nizamud Din, Abdul Aziz Batt, Musa Batt". Nizamud Din is also the name of one of his brothers. — [BHL, p. 28](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- "The first group from Bhalesa that took admission in Bhadarwah School includes the following persons: 1; Malik Umar-ul-Din 2: Professor Abdul Aziz Bhat 3: Ghulam Rasool Azad 4: Sultan Muhammad Khan…". The same passage says the district's "first High School was also approved in the year 1924". — [BHD](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- "Then he passed his BA from Prince of Wales College Jammu in 1938 AD". — [BHD, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- Prince of Wales College, Jammu "was renamed as Govt. Gandhi Memorial College on 20th May 1948". Taken from search-result text; I did not fetch the page. — [Wikipedia: Govt. Gandhi Memorial Science College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govt._Gandhi_Memorial_Science_College); also [Jammu Virasat](https://jammuvirasat.com/interesting-things-to-know-about-govt-gandhi-memorial-science-college-jammu-erstwhile-prince-of-wales-college)
- "…in his own words, 'Allah miraculously bestowed him with the chances to complete his MA Arabic and LLB course from Aligarh Muslim University'. Upon the suggestion of Ghulam-al-Syedain, he passed the exam of B.T. (Bachelor's in Teaching) in high grades. He also cleared Munshi Fazil exam in First Class". — [BHD, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- [BHL] gives his degrees as "M.A, B.T, L.L.B, H.P., E.E.T&P (London)". — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- Government Degree College, Bhaderwah was "established in 1955", when Bakshi Ghulam Mohammed laid its foundation. The Jammu University affiliation list also gives 1955. Taken from search-result text. — [GDC Bhadarwah, About](https://gdcbhadarwah.ac.in/About); [Jammu University affiliation list](https://jammuuniversity.ac.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/All%20NEW%20LIST%20%28New%29_0.pdf)
- "Principal of Bhadarwah Degree College" in 1956. — [BHD, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)

### Inferences
- The family memory of "Bhaderwah Degree College" probably blends his Bhaderwah high-school years with his later post as Principal of the college. This is my inference.
- The family's "LLB from AMU" is consistent with [BHD], which also gives an MA in Arabic from AMU. [BHD]'s "in his own words" means the source is his own account, not independent confirmation.

### Gaps
- No AMU alumni list, AMU convocation record or AMU calendar was found or searched successfully. The AMU LLB rests on one secondary source.
- The meaning of "H.P." and "E.E.T&P (London)" in [BHL]'s degree list is unexplained.

## Q4. Career before 1947: Hari Singh High School principal? GGM Science College lecturer?

### Takeaway
Neither claim was found. The record shows him entering state service in 1941 as Second Teacher at Kishtwar High School. The 1947 Civil List appears to show him as Head Master, Government High School, Kishtwar. [BHL] gives an early lectureship at Intermediate College, Mirpur.

### Cited Findings
- "…he got appointed as the Second-Teacher in Kishtwar High School in the year 1941 AD". The next lines are OCR-damaged and mention "Inspector of Schools at the same place" and "Lecturer and Headmaster at Mirpur". — [BHD, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- Date of entry into Government service "23-2-1998" (Bikrami, about May–June 1941). Promotion to present grade "17-12-2001" (about March 1945). — [CL-127](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481)
- In [CL-1947], Abdul Aziz Butt is entry 34, the last name in Kashmir Education Service Class III. The facing "Present charge" page begins "III (concluded)" with one post: "125 … Head Master, [Govern]ment High [School], Kishtwar." — [CL-1947](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.449300)
- "One of his earliest postings was as Lecturer at Intermediate College, Mirpur where among the students was Krishen Dev Sethi". — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- Hari Singh High School existed in Jammu city around 1940. A memoir notes three high schools in Jammu City: "Sri Ranbir High School, Hari Singh High school and Akabar Islamia High School". No headmaster is named. Taken from search-result text. — [Daily Excelsior, "When Passing Matriculation meant a Lot"](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/?p=1694950)

### Inferences
- The Kishtwar headmastership in early 1947 comes from matching column positions across facing pages of OCR text. It is probable, not certain. [BHD]'s 1941 Kishtwar appointment supports it.
- The Civil List covers government servants. If Hari Singh High School was not a government school, a headship there would not appear in it. I did not confirm the school's status.

### Gaps
- No record ties him to Hari Singh High School or to a lectureship at Prince of Wales / GGM College.
- No Mirpur Intermediate College staff record was found to date the lectureship.

## Q5. 1947 Jammu violence, daughter, wife, move to Srinagar

### Takeaway
The outline is supported. [BHD] says he and his wife **Syeda** were in a Pakistan-bound caravan from Jammu attacked on 5–6 November 1947, that "his daughter fell prey to the bullets of rioters", and that the couple survived. [BHL] says "He lost her daughter in turmoil". Neither source names the daughter (Masooda) or says she was an infant.

### Cited Findings
- OCR-damaged text from [BHD], p. 774, legible fragments only: "During the 1947 AD riots, he along with his wife" … "Syeda" … "were to-be taken t[o] Paki[stan as] part of the caravan of … from Jammu but were killed by some extremists on 5[th] and 6 November 1947 AD" … "at some distance from Jammu. Although his daughter fell Prey to the bullets of rioters, the couple … miraculously." It goes on: "Aziz Sahib writes in his own …" … "members including children were killed at … during the communal riots of Jammu. Materi[al]s worth lakhs of rupees were also plundered." — [BHD, pp. 774–775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- "He was the witness of many political upheavals in the state during partition and become prey of it." "He lost her daughter in turmoil." — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- "A few years ago, his wife Syeda Ji passed away … after a brief spell of illness." — [BHD, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- After 1947 he served as an Inspector of Schools in Anantnag ([BHD]: "…Inspector of Schools in Anantnag"). In [CL-127] (about 1952), the last post on the facing page, which by position matches his entry 19, is "Assistant Inspector of Schools, Anantnag" (Rs 215). — [BHD, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah); [CL-127](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481)
- Later residence: "Aziz lived in Gogjibagh area in Srinagar". — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)

### Inferences
- "Syeda" matches the family's "Saideh/Saida" as a spelling variant.
- The OCR phrase "were killed by some extremists" most likely refers to the caravan, not the couple, because the same passage says the couple survived. The sentence is too damaged to quote whole.
- After Partition he moved from Jammu-province postings (Kishtwar, Mirpur) to Kashmir-province postings (Anantnag), then to Srinagar. This fits the family's "later moved to Srinagar".

### Gaps
- The daughter's name (Masooda) and age were not found.
- The date of his wife's death is not given. [BHD] says only "a few years ago" relative to an undated Urdu original.
- No contemporaneous 1947 record (refugee or camp list, newspaper) naming him was found.

## Q6. University of Kashmir: founding faculty in 1948, Education curriculum, professorships, Dean, Registrar, PhD

### Takeaway
The Registrar role is supported by three sources, but the dates conflict: 1962–1969 and continuing after the 1969 split per [BHD], ended 1966 per [BHL], undated in [GK-2021]. None says "1970s" outright. The claims of founding faculty in 1948, building the Education curriculum, Dean of Education in the 1960s, professorships in English, Education or Social Work, and a 1970s PhD were **not found**. Several conflict with the documented timeline: he held school-cadre posts in 1947–53, the KU Education Department dates from 1965/1968 and is credited to Agha Ashraf Ali, and KU Social Work dates from 2003.

### Cited Findings
- **Registrar.** "He was later also appointed as the Deputy Director of Education Department Kashmir. After it, he graciously served the chair of Registrar Jammu and Kashmir University from 1962-1969 AD. After the branching out of Jammu University, he continued serving as a Registrar for Kashmir University. He retired from Government services in the year 1974 AD." OCR lightly normalised. — [BHD, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)
- **Registrar, conflicting end date.** "A dedicated and honest teacher, he rose to become registrar of University of Kashmir, a top job he relinquished in 1966." — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- **Registrar, undated.** "Professor Abdul Aziz served as the Registrar & Controller of the Jammu & Kashmir University, he was a great administrator." — [GK-2021](https://www.greaterkashmir.com/opinion/lest-we-forget-6/)
- **Other senior posts in the record.** "He also served as Chairman, Jammu & Kashmir Board of School Education, Principal, S. P. College and Inspector of Schools." "…he served as principal of MP School in 1956, 57 and later worked as principal SP College." — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- **The university in 1948.** The university was established in 1948 as the University of Jammu and Kashmir, "initially as an examining body", and split in 1969 into the University of Kashmir and the University of Jammu. Taken from search-result text. — [Wikipedia: University of Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kashmir)
- **First Registrar.** Khwaja Ghulam Ahmad Ashai was appointed Special Officer in 1948 and was the first Registrar and Chief Executive, 1948–1953. Paraphrased from the fetched column. — [GK-2021](https://www.greaterkashmir.com/opinion/lest-we-forget-6/)
- **Department founding dates (KU Prospectus 2021).** "DEPARTMENT OF EDUCATION / Year of Establishment: 1965"; "DEPARTMENT OF ENGLISH / Year of Establishment: 1954"; "DEPARTMENT OF SOCIAL WORK / Year of Establishment 2003". — [University of Kashmir Prospectus 2021 (Education on p. 32)](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/Download/Prospectus2021.pdf)
- **Who founded KU Education.** Agha Ashraf Ali: "His efforts led to the setting up of Department of Education at the University of Kashmir." "…started heading the Education Department in 1968." — [Kashmir Life, 26 Aug 2020](https://kashmirlife.net/agha-ashraf-ali-1922-2020-issue-21-vol-12-243771/). [GK-2021](https://www.greaterkashmir.com/opinion/lest-we-forget-6/) calls Agha Ashraf Ali the founder Dean and Head of the Post Graduate Department of Education (paraphrase). The same column lists later Education heads (Moti Lal Lidhoo, G R Abdullah, M L Wangoo, Abdul Rehman Rather, Salamatullah). Abdul Aziz appears only as Registrar.
- **School cadre in 1947–53.** He is listed in the Kashmir Education Service (Class III, headmasters and assistant inspectors) in 1947, about 1952 and about 1953. — [CL-1947](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.449300); [CL-127](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481); [CL-128](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.471671)
- **Degrees listed (no PhD).** M.A. (Arabic), LL.B., B.T., Munshi Fazil per [BHD]; "M.A, B.T, L.L.B, H.P., E.E.T&P (London)" per [BHL]. — [BHD, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah); [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)
- **Later education work.** "After the formation of College of Education at Sopore by Muslim Education Trust, he dedicatedly worked as the Principal of this college for the next five years. In the year 1986 AD, he laid the foundation of College of Education in Model Institute of Education Jammu and was appointed as the Advisor of the Director of the College Mr Arvin Gupta." — [BHD, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah). [BHL] dates the Sopore post 1974–1981. — [BHL, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)

### Inferences
- **"Founding faculty in 1948" is not supported and fits poorly with the timeline.** In 1948 the university was an examining body, and the Civil Lists keep him in the state school cadre through about 1953. His university role on record is administrative (Registrar, 1960s), not founding faculty.
- **"Built the Education curriculum from the rock"** is not supported for the University of Kashmir, where Agha Ashraf Ali is credited. His documented role in teacher education is as founding or early Principal of the MET College of Education, Sopore (after 1974) and as founder of the College of Education at Model Institute of Education, Jammu (1986). The family phrase may refer to these. This is my inference; neither source uses the phrase.
- **Professor of Social Work at KU is ruled out**: the department was founded in 2003, about 29 years after he retired in 1974. English (1954) and Education (1965) existed in his working years, but no source puts him on their faculty. The "Professor" title in the books may be honorific or reflect college principalships.
- **"Registrar in the 1970s"**: [BHD]'s "continued serving as a Registrar for Kashmir University" after the 1969 split could reach into the early 1970s, up to his 1974 retirement. [BHD] gives no end date, and [BHL] says 1966. The question is open.
- **PhD**: two independent-looking degree lists omit a PhD, which weakly counts against the claim. A Shodhganga search found nothing.

### Gaps
- No University of Kashmir or J&K University document naming him as Registrar was found, nor any Dean or faculty appointment. A university calendar or a list of past Registrars would resolve the 1962–1969 vs 1966 vs 1970s conflict.
- No PhD record was found on Shodhganga or in any library catalogue.
- The family phrase "from the rock" was not found in any source.
