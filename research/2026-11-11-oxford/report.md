# "Travelling scholar at Oxford": what the record holds, 11 November 2026

**Provenance.**
- A research subagent did this research on 11 November 2026. The parent agent saved the subagent's final report here, because the subagent could not write files.
- The parent agent checked three citations against the sources: F6, both lines (the 1970 committee in `dli.csl.1973`, and the 1975–76 Sopore line in `dli.ernet.509430`); and the C3 Hansard quotation of 17 May 1960. All three were found as quoted.
- The other citations are as the subagent reported them and have not been checked again.
- The family statement that he was a travelling scholar at Oxford (11 Nov 2026) is treated as true throughout. This research looked for a year, a college or a certificate. It was not testing the statement.

## Summary

- No source found names Abdul Aziz Butt (Bhat, Batt) at Oxford, as a travelling scholar, or in any British award list. That absence is not evidence against the family statement.
- Two sources give his only documented stay in Britain: a one-year course in England in about 1956. Neither names an institution or Oxford, so this report does not treat it as the Oxford visit.
- "H.P." among his degree letters is almost certainly Honours in Persian (Munshi Fazil), not a British award. "E.E.T&P (London)" remains unexplained.
- Two new records of his later posts turned up along the way (F6).

Labels used below:
- **Found:** a source names him, or, where stated, Ghulam Rasool Azad, who was on the 1956 course with him.
- **Context:** how the schemes of the time worked.
- **Lead:** something worth requesting.

## Context

- **C1. H.P. is Honours in Persian (Munshi Fazil).**
  - Punjab University Calendar 1929–30: "Munshi Fazil—Honours in Persian" (<https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.102739>).
  - Gazette of India 1973, No. 511: "Honours in Persian Language and Literature, to be called 'MUNSHI FAZIL'" (<https://archive.org/details/in.gazette.1973.511>).
  - J&K Civil List No. 127 prints "M. A., M. O. L., H. P." after a Persian scholar's name.
  - BHD says he "cleared Munshi Fazil exam in First Class".
  - **Inference:** H.P. and Munshi Fazil are the same qualification.
- **C2. E.E.T&P (London).** No source expands it. It may relate to the 1956 course, but no source links them. Only the certificate or the deputation order can settle it.
- **C3. Schemes that existed at the time.** None has been shown to have carried him.
  - **Commonwealth Teacher Training Bursary Scheme (from 1960).**
    - Bursars were placed by "a Commonwealth bursary unit representing the education departments, the Commonwealth Relations Office, the Colonial Office and the British Council".
    - Courses lasted "a year or perhaps in some cases two years or even longer".
    - On Oxford: "The authorities there are very much concerned about how they are to house teachers under this scheme who wish to go to Oxford for training."
    - Source: HC Deb 17 May 1960, <https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1960/may/17/commonwealth-teachers-bill>. The quotation was checked by the parent agent.
  - **The 1959 Oxford Commonwealth Education Conference.** It committed to "500 more places for teacher-trainees" and "a small number of Visiting Fellowships" (HL Deb 29 July 1959).
  - **Commonwealth Scholarship and Fellowship Plan (from 1960).** It offered "Fellowships over one" academic year (Atkinson, ERIC ED112453).
  - **Imperial Relations Trust Fellowships.** These were held at the Institute of Education, London, not Oxford, and India was included (UCL IE/OSD/2/A/11).
  - **British Council.**
    - Its scholarship files (BW 84) cover only 1937–48. Some individual bursary files exist for later years, e.g. BW 38/10 (1958–59).
    - A Commonwealth Relations Office file, DO 35/9483 (1957–60), is titled "Possibility of British Council operating on limited scale in Indian occupied Kashmir". **Inference:** the Council had no regular presence in J&K then.
  - **Other schemes:**
    - the Government of India Overseas Scholarship Scheme (for example, H. S. S. Lawrence, who went to Columbia in 1948);
    - Colombo Plan training places in the UK;
    - the Spalding Trust, whose focus on religion makes it a poor fit.
    - Rockefeller and UNESCO fellowships were not researched in depth.
- **C4. "Travelling scholar."** No Oxford status by that name was found.
  - **Inference:** visitors under these schemes were attached to the Oxford Institute or Department of Education and did not matriculate. Their names would therefore sit in departmental or British Council files, not in matriculation registers.
  - The Ministry of Education's files on the Oxford Institute of Education are TNA ED 159/77, /78, /141 and /142 (1951–61).

## Leads and where to ask

1. **Family papers.** A certificate, his passport for 1955–57 or 1960–62, or his service book, which normally records foreign deputations. The Azad family may hold matching papers for 1956.
2. **The J&K deputation order of 1955–56.** Also the J&K Gazette for 1955–57 and Civil Lists No. 130 onward. Ask the J&K Archives in Srinagar (Old Secretariat) or Jammu (Mubarak Mandi); see <https://jkarchives.nic.in>.
3. **The National Archives, Kew.**
   - OD 3/5, "Commonwealth Teaching Training Bursary Scheme: India", 1960–65 (open).
   - BW 38/19 and BW 38/22, the British Council India Representative's annual reports, 1955–61.
   - BW 38/20, staff visits in India, 1956–60.
   - DO 35/9483, the British Council and Kashmir.
   - ED 159/141 and /142, the Oxford Institute of Education.
4. **Oxford University Archives** (university.archives@bodleian.ox.ac.uk).
   - Ask a specific question about the Department of Educational Studies records for 1955–62.
   - The card register of students B–F (RS 42) is closed. The series has no cards for surnames beginning with A, so ask which students it covers.
   - The present Department of Education may also hold its own records.
5. **UCL Institute of Education Archives**, if the London course matters.
   - IE/COL/20/1, Commonwealth bursars 1960–66, which includes lists of bursars.
   - IE/OSD/2/A/2, IRT Fellowships for India 1952–76, which includes applications.
6. **Abhilekh Patal (National Archives of India).** It blocks automated search. Search the Ministry of Education and Ministry of External Affairs files for 1955–62 by hand.
7. **Cmnd. 841, the report of the Oxford Commonwealth Education Conference (1959).** It lists the delegations, though a state officer is unlikely to appear.
8. **The Commonwealth Scholarship Commission**, for awards from 1960 to 1962.
9. **India Office Records.** Little to expect here. The only name hit, IOR/L/PJ/7/14789, was a different person.
