## 1. What statute, order, or notification created the university, on what date, and under what name (including the University of Jammu and Kashmir phase)?

### Takeaway
All sources agree on a 1948 origin, and one gives an inauguration date of 2 November 1948. I could not find or open the founding instrument itself, so its title, number and date are still unverified. Official accounts describe two stages. In 1948 the state set up an examining body at Srinagar. A teaching "full-fledged University" called the University of Jammu and Kashmir followed in 1956 (one source says 1958). The university was reconstituted under the Jammu and Kashmir University Act, 1965.

### Cited Findings
- The current official page says: "The University of Jammu and Kashmir was founded in the year 1948." — [UoK-live](https://kashmiruniversity.ac.in/AboutUOK.aspx)
- The 2013 official history describes the 1948 body as an examining institute, not yet a full university: "The University arose out of humble origins immediately after Independence when in 1948 the State Government established an institute for conduct of examinations with its headquarters at Srinagar, Justice J. N. Wazir being its Honorary Vice-Chancellor." — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- The same page dates the university name to the 1956 teaching phase. After describing the 1956 postgraduate departments it says: "Thus came into being a full-fledged University, then christerned [sic] the University of Jammu and Kashmir, with Mr. A. A. Fyzee as its first whole-time Vice-Chancellor." — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- Greater Kashmir (2015) says the university was set up because the Punjab University at Lahore, to which J&K's institutions had been affiliated, "became inaccessible" after 1947. "The last examinations were conducted by Punjab University Lahore in 1947. No University examinations were conducted in Jammu and Kashmir in 1948." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Greater Kashmir (2015) on the founding instrument: Ghulam Ahmad Ashai "was chosen as Special Officer to draft the Act, statutes and regulations for the proposed University and constitute different authorities – the Senate, Syndicate, Academic Council and the Faculties. He took over this job in July 1948 and accomplished it in record time. The University was inaugurated on November 2, 1948." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Greater Kashmir (2015) uses the name University of Jammu and Kashmir for the 1948 body: "The University of Jammu and Kashmir was just an affiliating and examining body when it was established in 1948 and became a teaching University in 1958." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- 1965 reconstitution. The official history says: "In 1965 the University was reorganized and divided into two divisions of equal status; one for the province of Jammu and the other for the province of Kashmir - with a central organization having specific legislative and executive powers." It also refers to "the statutes and regulations of the Jammu and Kashmir University Act, 1965". — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- The 1969 Act, s.52, confirms that the 1965 statute was the immediate legal basis before the split. It refers to "the University of Jammu and Kashmir constituted under the Jammu and Kashmir University Act, 1965". — [Act-1969 s.52 (Lawgist)](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/52)
- **Conflict on the year teaching began (and with it, when it became a "full" university):** official [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx) says 1956; [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/) says 1958. The official university page is the more authoritative of the two.
- **Conflict on when the name was used:** [UoK-live](https://kashmiruniversity.ac.in/AboutUOK.aspx) and [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/) apply "University of Jammu and Kashmir" to the 1948 body. [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx) calls the 1948 body "an institute for conduct of examinations" and says it was "then christerned [sic] the University of Jammu and Kashmir" after 1956.

### Inferences
- Greater Kashmir says Ashai drafted "the Act" in July 1948, before the 2 November 1948 inauguration. So some 1948 legislative instrument probably existed, perhaps a Samvat-dated Act or ordinance of the Maharaja's or the Emergency Administration's government. I have not seen its title, number or date, and it should not be cited by name.
- The two official accounts can be reconciled. A 1948 examining and affiliating university (its legal name possibly already "University of Jammu and Kashmir") gained teaching departments in 1956/58. It was then reconstituted by the 1965 Act. The 2013 page's "then christened" wording may describe the teaching university rather than a renaming. This is my reading, not a sourced fact.

### Gaps
- I did not find the 1948 founding statute, ordinance or notification: its title, number, Samvat or Gregorian date, or gazette citation. A search for "Jammu and Kashmir University Act" with Samvat 2005 returned nothing. Next places to look: the J&K Archives, the J&K Government Gazette for 1948, and the University Calendar's historical preface.
- I did not open the text of the Jammu and Kashmir University Act, 1965 (number, assent date).
- It is unclear whether the 1948 inauguration on 2 November 1948 (GK-2015) matches a gazette date. No official source I opened gives a day and month for 1948.

## 2. When did teaching, examinations, and the first convocation begin? Which colleges or campuses were the first seat?

### Takeaway
The first examinations were in March 1949. Postgraduate teaching began in 1956 according to the official account (1958 according to Greater Kashmir), with English at Srinagar and Economics and Geology at Jammu. The first convocation was held in Municipal Park (now Sher-i-Kashmir Park), Srinagar, but no source I opened gives its year. Early teaching ran out of affiliated colleges and a rented building at Haft Chinar, Srinagar.

### Cited Findings
- First examinations: "The first examinations were conducted by this University in March 1949." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Students admitted in 1948 without a qualifying examination: "they were admitted to the first year of Intermediate course and to the third year of Degree course, without having passed the qualifying examination, on the basis of a test conducted by the principals of local colleges." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Official account of the start of teaching: "the establishment of three postgraduate departments in 1956 - English at Srinagar, and Economics and Geology at Jammu." Also: "Since 1956, this academic institution has come a long way and has developed into a multi-faculty University." — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- Greater Kashmir's account of the start of teaching (dated 1958): it "became a teaching University in 1958 by taking over the three post-graduate departments – English, Economics and Geology from two affiliated colleges and adding the departments of Urdu, Hindi and Mathematics. English was till then taught in S.P.College Srinagar while Economics and Geology were attached to Prince of Wales College Jammu (now Government Gandhi Memorial)." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- First teaching premises: "All these departments were housed in a rented building at Haft Chinar, Srinagar except the department of Geology which was retained for some time in Gandhi College for lack of necessary infrastructure in the rented building." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- First convocation venue: "After the first convocation was held in Municipal Park (now Sheri Kashmir Park) the venue was shifted to Raj Garh (Sher Garhi) till the University acquired its own campus in Hazratbal." Speakers at the early convocations included "Dr. Rajendra Prasad, Dr. Zakir Hussain, Dr. Radha Krishnan, Ali Asghar Hekmat, Clovis Masood, SS Bhatnagar." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Greater Kashmir (2015) was written to correct an earlier Greater Kashmir convocation article. It objects that "It is not, therefore, correct to say that it was Kashmir University which celebrated the convocation in 1951," because the 1951 institution was the J&K University. It disputes the institution's name, not the 1951 date. — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Seat: the 1948 examining institute had "its headquarters at Srinagar". — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)

### Inferences
- A convocation took place in 1951. The earlier Greater Kashmir article said so, and Chisti's correction accepts the date and disputes only the name. That 1951 convocation may have been the first, but no source I opened says "first convocation, 1951".
- Before 1956/58 the university taught nothing itself. It examined and affiliated, and teaching took place in colleges such as S.P. College, Srinagar and Prince of Wales College, Jammu.

### Gaps
- The exact date and year of the first convocation are not stated in any source I opened. The Wikipedia Ashai article has a photo captioned as the first convocation procession. That caption is unsourced, and I did not use it.
- I did not open a University Calendar or official convocation record giving the first convocation date or the number of graduates.
- The month and year in 1956 (or 1958) when the postgraduate departments opened are not given.

## 3. Who were the first chancellor, vice-chancellor, registrar, and other founding officers named in primary or official sources, with years?

### Takeaway
The first Registrar was Ghulam Ahmad Ashai, then Principal of S.P. College. He was Special Officer from July 1948 and Registrar from the 2 November 1948 inauguration (GK-2015). The first Vice-Chancellor is disputed. The official 2013 page names Justice J. N. Wazir as the 1948 Honorary Vice-Chancellor. Greater Kashmir (2015) names Justice Kazi Masud Hasan as the first honorary VC, followed by Wazir. A. A. A. Fyzee was the first whole-time (full-time) VC. No source I opened names the first Chancellor. The official page calls Dr. Karan Singh "the second Chancellor".

### Cited Findings
- **Vice-Chancellor (official version):** "...in 1948 the State Government established an institute for conduct of examinations with its headquarters at Srinagar, Justice J. N. Wazir being its Honorary Vice-Chancellor." — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- **Vice-Chancellor (Greater Kashmir version):** "Till 1958 the University had honorary Vice-Chancellors, the first being Justice Kazi Masud Hasan, Judge State High Court, followed by Justice Wazir Janki Nath, Chief Justice." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- **Conflict:** [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx) (official) gives J. N. Wazir as the 1948 Honorary VC. [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/) (a reader's reminiscence) puts Kazi Masud Hasan first and Wazir second. The official page is the more authoritative source. Greater Kashmir is more specific, though, and the official page may simply not mention a short first term. The Wikipedia VC list gives "Qazi Masood Hassan (1948-1949)" and "Justice J. N. Wazir (1949-1957)". It cites the Greater Kashmir piece, but that piece gives no such years, so the year ranges are unsupported by the cited source.
- **First whole-time VC:** "Mr. A. A. Fyzee as its first whole-time Vice-Chancellor", in the 1956 phase. — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx). Greater Kashmir: "Asaf Ali Asgar Fyzee ... joined as the first full-time Vice-Chancellor. He was succeeded by ... Sardar K.M. Panikkar." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/). Note the possible conflict: the official page ties Fyzee to the 1956 phase, while Greater Kashmir says VCs were honorary "till 1958".
- **Registrar:** "Khawja Ghulam Ahmad Ashai, Principal S.P.College, ... was chosen as Special Officer ... He took over this job in July 1948 ... The University was inaugurated on November 2, 1948 and Ashai Sahib was appointed its first Registrar." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- **Chancellor:** the official page mentions only "Dr. Karan Singh, the second Chancellor of the University", who donated the campus land. — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- **Pro-Vice-Chancellor, after the 1965 Act:** "Mr. G. A. Mukhtar, the then Education Advisor to the State Government was appointed the first Pro-Vice-Chancellor of the Kashmir division." — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- **University of Jammu after the split:** its first VC is given as "Prof J N Bhan (Sept 1969- Dec 1973)". — [DE-2012](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/news/archive/from-past-to-present)
- **Chancellor under the 1969 Act:** the Governor is Chancellor of both universities and the Chief Minister is ex officio Pro-Chancellor. I took this from search-result summaries of the Act. I did not open the relevant sections (ss.10–11), so it needs checking against the Act text.

### Inferences
- If Karan Singh was the "second Chancellor", the first Chancellor was presumably the head of state before him. The obvious candidate is Maharaja Hari Singh, but that is a hypothesis to check, not a finding. No source I opened names him, and he should not be named as first Chancellor without a source.
- Most early VCs were sitting or senior judges (Kazi Masud Hasan, J. N. Wazir) serving in an honorary capacity. That fits an institution that, before 1956/58, mainly examined and affiliated.

### Gaps
- No source I opened names the first Chancellor or gives the year.
- I have no official VC list with years. The official formerVCs page did not render its list in the HTML I retrieved.
- Ashai's Registrar term end date: the Wikipedia article says 1948–1953 without a source. The Greater Kashmir commemorative article (13 March 2015) that might support it could not be fetched.
- I found no named founding Treasurer, Controller of Examinations, or Senate or Syndicate members.

## 4. When and by what law was it split into the University of Kashmir and the University of Jammu?

### Takeaway
The Governor promulgated an ordinance on 5 September 1969. It was replaced by the Kashmir and Jammu Universities Act, 1969, which received the Governor's assent on 30 October 1969 and was published in the Government Gazette on 10 November 1969. The Act created the "University of Kashmir" (Kashmir Division) and the "University of Jammu" (Jammu Division) in place of the University of Jammu and Kashmir constituted under the J&K University Act, 1965.

### Cited Findings
- Official account: "By an ordinance promulgated by the Governor of the State of J&K on 5 September 1969 - which was subsequently replaced by an Act of the State Legislature and was published in the Government Gazette on 10 November 1969, the University of Jammu and Kashmir was bifurcated into two full-fledged Universities : the University of Jammu and the University of Kashmir." — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- Current official page: "In the year 1969 it was bifurcated into two full-fledged Universities", the University of Kashmir at Srinagar and the University of Jammu at Jammu. — [UoK-live](https://kashmiruniversity.ac.in/AboutUOK.aspx)
- Daily Excelsior (2012), on the University of Jammu: the Governor "promulgated an ordinance on 5th Sept 1969". It was later ratified by the State legislature, and the Act "received the assent of the Governor on 30th October, 1969". The University of Jammu "started functioning from the Jammu Division of erstwhile University of Jammu & Kashmir", next to GGM Science College, and the permanent campus later went to Bahu-Wali Rakh. "At the time of inception the university had eleven PG teaching departments." — [DE-2012](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/news/archive/from-past-to-present)
- Short title, s.1: "This Act may be called the Kashmir and Jammu Universities Act, 1969." — [Act-1969 s.1 (Lawgist)](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/1)
- Section 3: "(1) There shall be two Universities in the * State, one in the Kashmir Division and the other in the Jammu Division, each having jurisdiction extending to its respective Division ... (2) The University in the Kashmir Division shall be known as the 'University of Kashmir' and the University in the Jammu Division shall be known as 'University of Jammu'." — [Act-1969 s.3 (Lawgist)](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/3)
- Section 52 deals with transition. Employees of "the University of Jammu and Kashmir constituted under the Jammu and Kashmir University Act, 1965" continue in service, and the Chancellor may allocate them "between the University of Kashmir and the University of Jammu". The staff, assets and liabilities of the old university's Publication Bureau go to the J&K Board of Secondary Education. — [Act-1969 s.52 (Lawgist)](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/52)
- The dates agree across sources: ordinance 5 Sept 1969 (UoK-2013, DE-2012); assent 30 Oct 1969 (DE-2012); Gazette 10 Nov 1969 (UoK-2013). The most primary source would be the Act's own assent and gazette line, but I could not open it (India Code PDF 403; jklaw.nic.in unreachable).

### Inferences
- The two universities came into being legally under the 5 September 1969 ordinance. The Act of 30 October/10 November 1969 replaced it. Official histories date the bifurcation to "1969" without saying which instrument they mean.

### Gaps
- The Act number: "Act No. XXIV of 1969" appears in an India Code listing title and in search snippets, but I could not open the India Code or jklaw PDF to confirm it from the text.
- The ordinance's title and number are not given in any source I opened.
- The repeal and savings clause (whether it expressly repeals the 1965 Act) is unread.
- I did not open the University of Jammu's own official history page, so its account is represented only through the Daily Excelsior article.

## 5. What do official histories say about the first premises? (Record "from the rock" only if a source uses it.)

### Takeaway
No source I opened uses the phrase "from the rock", so it is not recorded as a sourced phrase. The official history speaks of "humble origins" and an examining institute with "its headquarters at Srinagar". Greater Kashmir (2015) gives the details. The Registrar's office was in a government bungalow at Lal Mandi. Teaching departments were in a rented building at Haft Chinar. Early convocations were held in Municipal Park and later at Raj Garh (Sher Garhi). Several campus sites were tried before Dr. Karan Singh donated the Hazratbal land (called "Amar Singh Bagh" in the official account and "Raja Bagh" in Greater Kashmir). Construction there began in 1959.

### Cited Findings
- "From the rock": the phrase does not occur in [UoK-live](https://kashmiruniversity.ac.in/AboutUOK.aspx), [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx), [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/) or [DE-2012](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/news/archive/from-past-to-present). The official motto is translated "From Darkness to Light". — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- Official wording on origins: "The University arose out of humble origins immediately after Independence when in 1948 the State Government established an institute for conduct of examinations with its headquarters at Srinagar." — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- Registrar's office: "the office of the Registrar which was housed in Lal Mandi in a Government bungalow was shifted from there to the College of Education in early 1953 to facilitate the process of acquiring the proposed site. The proposal, however, fell through in August the same year and the Registrar's office was shifted back to Lal Mandi." — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Teaching departments: "housed in a rented building at Haft Chinar, Srinagar". Geology stayed for a time in Gandhi College, Jammu. — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Sites considered:
  - Under Sheikh Abdullah as Prime Minister: "the entire area on the hotel road (now Maulana Azad Road) from Pratap Bagh to Maulana Azad bridge", a proposal that "fell through in August" 1953.
  - "During the regime of Bakhshi Sahib Hazuri Bagh (now Iqbal Park) was chosen for the campus and even the foundation stone of its first building was laid by Dr. S. Radha Krishnan ... this site was abandoned, the foundation stone being stolen."
  - "Finally Dr.Karan Singh donated one of his orchards, Raja Bagh, near Hazratbal which was approved for the campus."
  — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- Official account of the Hazratbal campus: "The University was shifted to the present campus, known as Amar Singh Bagh, when the land was donated by Dr. Karan Singh, the second Chancellor of the University." The layout plan was by "Mr. Fayaz-ud-Din, Chief Town Planner from what is now Andhra Pradesh and Mr. G. B. Mhatra, an eminent architect of Bombay." "...construction works began at the Campus in 1959, with the foundation stone laid for the Arts Faculty Block by Bakhshi Ghulam Mohammad, the then Prime Minister of the State, on 5 June 1959. The Faculty was commissioned on 1 August 1960 by Dr. S. Radhakrishnan, the then Vice-President of the country." — [UoK-2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)
- Greater Kashmir agrees: "The first building on this site – the Arts Faculty building – came up in early 1960s", opened by Dr. S. Radhakrishnan. Naseem Bagh and Mirza Bagh were acquired later. — [GK-2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)
- **Conflict on the campus name:** the official page calls the donated land "Amar Singh Bagh" and Greater Kashmir calls the donated orchard "Raja Bagh, near Hazratbal". The official page is the more authoritative. The two names may refer to the same land or to parts of it; neither source settles this.

### Inferences
- The early "premises" were scattered borrowed or rented spaces: a government bungalow for administration, affiliated colleges and a rented building for teaching, public parks and palace grounds for convocations. The university had its own campus only from about 1959–60.

### Gaps
- No source I opened says where the 1948 examining institute's headquarters stood (building or street) beyond "Srinagar". Lal Mandi is named only for the Registrar's office, and only by Greater Kashmir.
- "From the rock": not found in any source opened. If a later writer has seen it attributed to the university, its source needs to be identified and opened before it is used.
- When the Registrar's office and departments moved from Lal Mandi and Haft Chinar to Hazratbal is not dated beyond the 1959 start of construction and the 1960 commissioning of the Arts block.
