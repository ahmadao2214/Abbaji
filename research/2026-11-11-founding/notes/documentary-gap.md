# University of Kashmir founding history: the documentary gap around "Abdul Aziz Butt/Bhat" (family dates 1916–2016)

Research date: 2026-11-11. All pages listed below were opened during this session. Wikipedia articles were read as raw wikitext (`action=raw`) on that date. Scanned PDFs were read from their OCR text layer and, for the two key pages, from the page images themselves.

**Headline for the report writer:** the university's own published *University Calendar 2010, Vol. I* (pp. xviii–xix, "History and Growth of the University – An Overview", "Succession List") lists **"4. Prof. Abdul Aziz — Sept1962 – 1971"** under **Registrars**. It gives **no surname, no father's name and no other identifying detail**. So the published record does name a Registrar called Abdul Aziz in exactly the right period. What it does *not* show is that he is the family's Abdul Aziz Butt/Bhat (1916–2016). That link is still unproven. The family's other belief, that he was "responsible for the education programme", has no support in anything I found. The one published period source names **Dr. Ashraf Agha** as "Head of Post-graduate Department of Education" in 1966.

---

## Q1. Which names recur in official histories, jubilee volumes, and Wikipedia's citations as founders or first officers? (Quoted lists)

### Takeaway
Three kinds of source name founders and early officers. The university's own pages (2007/2013 "About" text and the 2010 Calendar's Succession List) name early Chancellors, Pro-Chancellors, Vice-Chancellors, Registrars and benefactors. English Wikipedia's founding statements and its Registrar caption carry **no supporting citation**. Names that recur: Justice J. N. Wazir, A. A. A. Fyzee, Karan Singh, Sheikh Mohammad Abdullah, Bakhshi Ghulam Mohammad, G. A. (Ghulam Ahmad) Ashai and G. A. Mukhtar. I found no jubilee volume.

### Inferences
- The Calendar's Succession List is the best published, university-issued record of first officers. It matches the Ashai Wikipedia article on Ashai's tenure (1948–1953). It is a 2010 compilation, though, not a record made at the time, and it gives no sources for its dates.
- "Founders" in the published record means office-holders and patrons: Chancellor, Pro-Chancellor, VC, Registrar, land donor and foundation-stone layers. No source I found lists a "founding committee" or founding staff below Registrar level.
- Registrar #4 served across both the 1965 federal reorganisation and the 1969 bifurcation. The 1966 Review takes subscriptions through "The Joint-Registrar, Jammu and Kashmir University, Kashmir Division Srinagar". So during 1965–69 the Kashmir Division apparently had its own Joint Registrar, while the "Registrar" may have been a central post. The Calendar compresses this into one continuous line. Which post he actually held in each year needs documents.

### Gaps
- **No jubilee volume found.** I found no Golden Jubilee (1998/2006) or Diamond Jubilee souvenir from KU or JU online. Searches surfaced none, and the KU site has no publications archive for one.
- The live KU history page (`history.aspx`) returned "Sorry an Error Occured". The live "Former Vice Chancellors" page and the Registrar details page ("Details Not Present at the moment") rendered no content. KU has **no "Former Registrars" web page**; the Calendar PDF is the only place the Registrar list appears. — [KU history.aspx](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/history.aspx); [KU formerVCs.aspx](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/formerVCs.aspx); [KU Registrar details](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/administrationdetails.aspx?link=58)
- I could not retrieve the Greater Kashmir 2015 article cited for Ashai (empty response), so I could not check what it supports.
- **Conflicts to flag:**
  - *First VC:* the Calendar lists "Qazi Masud Hasan 1948 – 1949", but the KU About text names J. N. Wazir as Honorary VC in 1948.
  - *Date of the 1969 Act in the Gazette:* the About page says "10 November 1969". The Calendar's reprint of the Act has a garbled OCR header that reads like "17th November, 1969". I did not check this against the image.
  - *Founding year:* 1948 (infobox, KU pages) vs 1956 (Wikipedia categories; KU's "full-fledged University" from 1956).

## Q2. Do those sources name the first Education teachers or deans, and the Registrars? Where do they omit years or people?

### Takeaway
**Registrars:** the 2010 Calendar has a full 19-name list (1948–2007+). Entry 4, "Prof. Abdul Aziz, Sept1962 – 1971", has **only a given name** and an end date given as a bare year. **Education:** no official source names the department's first teachers or deans. The only published name for an early Education head is **Dr. Ashraf Agha, "Head of Post-graduate Department of Education"**, in the 1966 *J&K University Review*. The department's own history gives dates but no names.

### Cited Findings
- KU Department of Education: "The Department of Education was established in the year 1965. In the beginning, one year M.Ed. course was offered and a two-year Master's Programme in Education (MA) was subsequently introduced in the year 1979. In the early eighties, the Department initiated research programme at M.Phil and Ph.D. levels. For the last 36 years about 252 scholars have been awarded the degrees of Ph.D and M.Phil in Education." **No founding staff, heads or deans are named.** — [KU Dept. of Education, About Us](https://education.uok.edu.in/Main/AboutUs.aspx)
- *J&K University Review* editorial (1964–66 volume): "The Post-graduate Departments of Education and History and Diploma Course in Public Administration have started functioning." The contributor list names "Dr. Ashraf Agha, Head of Post-graduate Department of Education", and he wrote "Russia Challenges American Education" in the Vol. VIII (June 1966) issue. — [Internet Archive dli.ernet.503903](https://archive.org/metadata/dli.ernet.503903)
- The 1966 issue also has a regular column, "What the Joint-Registrar Reports". This is the kind of in-house news column that would record appointments and transfers. In the OCR'd text I found **no occurrence of "Aziz"**, and no sentence naming who held the Registrar or Joint-Registrar post. — [Internet Archive dli.ernet.503903](https://archive.org/metadata/dli.ernet.503903)
- KU Calendar overview (OCR text): when "the University printed its Calendar for the first time" in 1972, the Faculty of Education offered "M. Ed. (one year course)". By 2010 the Faculty of Education listed M.A. Education, M.Ed, M.P.Ed, B.P.Ed and B.Ed. — [KU University Calendar](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/download/UniCal.pdf)
- Registrar list with its gaps and imprecisions: entry 4 has no surname, and its end date is a year with no month ("1971"). Entries 5–6 likewise use bare years (1971, 1975). Entry 2 ends "May1962" and entry 3 runs only "May1962 – Sept1962" (four months). Entry 16 begins "March1997", four months before entry 15 ends ("July 1997"), so those two tenures overlap. — [KU University Calendar, p. xix](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/download/UniCal.pdf)
- The 1950s statute book *The University Of Jammu And Kashmir* (DLI scan) lists the statutory officers as "The Chancellor; The Pro-Chancellor; The Vice-Chancellor; The Treasurer; The Registrar; and… such other officers as the Statutes may declare". It also names "Deans", "Proctor", "Librarian" and "Deputy Registrar" as offices, and states "The Sadr-i-Riyasat shall be the Chancellor". This book defines offices but does not list who held them. — [Internet Archive in.ernet.dli.2015.449006](https://archive.org/metadata/in.ernet.dli.2015.449006)
- The Kashmir and Jammu Universities Act 1969, as reprinted in the Calendar (OCR), gives transitional provisions. The "appointment of the first Vice-Chancellors, the first Registrars… of both the Universities… shall be made by the Chancellor in consultation with the Pro-Chancellor". It also provides for "Continuance of service of the existing employees and their allocation". — [KU University Calendar](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/download/UniCal.pdf)

### Inferences
- Under the 1969 Act, the Chancellor had to make a formal appointment of "the first Registrar" of the new University of Kashmir. The Calendar shows Prof. Abdul Aziz in office continuously until 1971. If he was KU's first Registrar under the Act, there should be a Chancellor's order or notification from late 1969 or 1970, plus a staff-allocation order between the two universities.
- Education was a post-graduate *department* from about 1964–65 (founding year 1965 per the department; a 1964–66 editorial says it had "started functioning"). The family's phrase "responsible for the education programme" could mean many things: the Education department, teacher-training affiliation, the examinations the Registrar ran, or something else entirely. The published record shows a separate person, Dr. Ashraf Agha, heading the Education department in 1966. So the Education claim needs its own document. It cannot rest on the Registrar listing.

### Gaps
- No source lists the Education department's founding faculty, its heads in order, or Deans of the Faculty of Education. I found no departmental history or anniversary souvenir.
- No source gives Prof. Abdul Aziz's surname, his department or subject (the "Prof." implies a teaching post), or what he did before or after 1962–1971.
- I could not check whether the JU side published a matching Registrar list. JU's website shows none.

## Q4. Where would appointment orders, calendars, annual reports, gazette notifications, convocation programmes and PhD registrations (1950s–1980s) be held now? Digitized vs. request-only

### Takeaway
Little is digitized: one KU Calendar (2010), annual reports from 2011 on, and a few 1950s–60s DLI scans (statutes, one convocation address, a 1964/1966 *Review* volume, a 1953 Gazette). Appointment orders, service files, Syndicate/Council minutes, convocation programmes and 1960s–80s registers are not online. They would have to be requested from KU's Registrar's office and the Allama Iqbal Library, and the relevant years of the J&K Government Gazette consulted.

### Cited Findings
- **Allama Iqbal Library (KU)**: "established in 1948 with the inception of the University of Jammu and Kashmir". After the 1969 split "these universities shared the li[brary]". It "houses… rare books, manuscripts, theses, dissertations, and newspaper archives". It has an "Online Public Access Catalogue (OPAC)" and has contributed to "National Digital Library of India… and Shodhganga". — [Allama Iqbal Library, About Us](https://ail.uok.edu.in/Main/AboutUs.aspx)
- The DLI scan of the 1964/1966 *J&K University Review* carries an "Allama Iqbal Library" stamp. That shows AIL held, and probably still holds, the *Review* run. — [Internet Archive dli.ernet.503903](https://archive.org/metadata/dli.ernet.503903)
- **Calendars:** KU publishes a *University Calendar* PDF (the 2010 Vol. I scan, which has some 2014 pages bound in). It says the University "printed its Calendar for the first time" in 1972 (OCR). — [KU University Calendar](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/download/UniCal.pdf)
- **Annual reports:** the KU DIQA page lists only "Annual Report 2011" through "Annual Report 2024-25". Nothing from before 2011 is online. — [KU DIQA Annual Reports](https://diqa.uok.edu.in/Main/ViewPage.aspx?Page=Annual_Report)
- **Statutes / Acts:** the 1950s statute book is digitized ([IA in.ernet.dli.2015.449006](https://archive.org/metadata/in.ernet.dli.2015.449006)). The 1969 Act is reprinted in the KU Calendar ([UniCal.pdf](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/download/UniCal.pdf)). Wikipedia cites an India Code PDF of the 1969 Act (not opened by me) and summarises the Registrar as the officer who "handles university records and property" and serves as secretary to the Council, Syndicate and Academic Council. — [Wikipedia: University of Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kashmir)
- **Gazettes:** J&K Government Gazette 1953 (5,870 pp.) and other DLI gazette volumes are on Internet Archive. In my search, 1962–1971 volumes did not show up. — [IA gov_gazette_1953](https://archive.org/metadata/gov_gazette_1953)
- **Convocation:** the 1955 Sixth Convocation Address is digitized. I found no convocation *programmes* (which list officers in the procession). — [IA dli.ernet.425381](https://archive.org/metadata/dli.ernet.425381)

## Q5. What evidence would support (a) a family memorial page, (b) a university-acknowledged profile, (c) a reference-work entry, without presenting oral history as published fact?

### Takeaway
