# Education at the University of Kashmir and the Office of the Registrar, 1948 to the 1980s

Scope note: this file tests the institutional timeline only. It does not assume that any family member held a post, and it uses no family memory. A name counts as a finding only when a source I opened ties it to a post. Search-engine snippets from pages I could not open are kept out of Cited Findings and listed under Gaps as leads.

## Q1. When was the Department, Faculty or College of Education set up, under what name, and did a government teachers' college come first?

### Takeaway
The university's own Education department page dates the **Department of Education to 1965**. It opened with a one-year M.Ed., added a two-year M.A. in 1979, and started M.Phil./Ph.D. research in the early 1980s. A **government teacher-training system came first**. Two training schools, in Srinagar and Jammu, existed before 1947. By the 1947–61 review period there were two government **post-graduate training colleges for teachers**, one in Srinagar and one in Jammu. Both predate the 1965 university department.

### Cited Findings
- The department page says "The Department of Education was established in the year 1965." It places the department under the School of Education & Behavioural Sciences. — [UoK Department of Education, About Us](https://education.uok.edu.in/Main/AboutUs.aspx)
- Wording conflict: the text I fetched says "Department of Education", with no "Faculty" or "College". A search-engine rendering of the same page read "Department/Faculty of Education was established in the year 1965". The exact 1965 name (Department or Faculty) should be checked against a calendar. — [UoK Department of Education, About Us](https://education.uok.edu.in/Main/AboutUs.aspx)
- Programmes on the same page:
  - "In the beginning, one year M.Ed. course was offered."
  - A two-year M.A. in Education began in **1979**.
  - "In the early eighties, the Department initiated research programme at M.Phil and Ph.D. levels."
  - A self-financed B.Ed. began in 2003.
  - No founder, head, dean or professor is named. — [UoK Department of Education, About Us](https://education.uok.edu.in/Main/AboutUs.aspx)
- The department was founded before the 1969 split. In 1965 it belonged to the single Jammu and Kashmir University. Wikipedia says "The inception of the University of Kashmir dates back to the establishment of Jammu and Kashmir University in 1948", and in 1969 the university "was made into two full-fledged universities" at Srinagar and Jammu. — [Wikipedia: University of Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kashmir)
- Wikipedia contradicts itself on the founding date. The text says 1948, but its categories include "Universities and colleges established in 1956". The History section gives no inline citation for 1948 or 1969. — [Wikipedia: University of Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kashmir)
- Government teacher training before the university department (Kashmir Life, 1 Sept 2022, reproducing *Review of Education in India: Jammu and Kashmir (1947–61)*, a Government of India Ministry of Education publication):
  - "Until 1947, there were only two training schools, one at Srinagar and the other at Jammu, with arrangements to train only 200 teachers."
  - For the later review period: "There are two post-graduate training colleges for teachers, one at Srinagar and the other at Jammu," with "total capacity … to train 250 teachers every year." — [Kashmir Life, "Schooling In 1947"](https://kashmirlife.net/schooling-in-1947-vol-14-issue-22-299084/)
- Dr Seema Naz, Principal of the Government College of Education Srinagar, interviewed by Syed Mohammad Burhan (5 Nov 2022):
  - "In its inception, it was a school. It was known as teacher training college."
  - "It was famously known as TT College."
  - "After TT College it was changed to B. Ed College."
  - A 2017 government order renamed it IASE, and the same year it was merged into Cluster University.
  - The interview gives no founding year. — [Kashmir Observer](https://kashmirobserver.net/?p=168715)

### Inferences
- Srinagar had a government post-graduate teacher-training college (the "TT College", later the B.Ed. College and Government College of Education) by about 1961 at the latest. The university's Department of Education (1965) was therefore a second, separate body. Government college staff and university department staff should not be treated as the same people.
- Because the department began in 1965 with a one-year M.Ed., the university's own Education teaching started at the post-graduate (M.Ed.) level. The B.Ed.-level teacher training stayed with the government college.
- A University Registrar between 1948 and 1965 could not have run a university Department of Education, because none existed yet. In those years Education teaching sat in the government training college.

### Gaps

College of Education in 1968". The site served only a bot-check page, so I could not open it. Treat 1937/1948/1968 as an unverified lead.
- A snippet from a sikhiwiki page (403, not opened) says a remodelled teachers' training school opened in Srinagar in October 1938, following the 1938 Education Reorganisation Committee chaired by K. G. Saiyidain, then Director of Education. Unverified. It also conflicts with the 1937 date.
- The government college's affiliation is unknown: when it began sending candidates to Jammu and Kashmir University examinations (B.T./B.Ed.), and under which faculty.
- The exact 1965 name (Department of Education or Faculty of Education) and the approving body (Syndicate or Academic Council resolution) were not found. The 1965–66 University Calendar or the Syndicate minutes would settle it.
- Whether a "Faculty of Education" existed before 1965 as a university examining body, separate from a teaching department, is unknown. That body would have examined the government college's B.T./B.Ed. candidates.

## Q3. Which Registrars are named, with years, and how does the Registrar differ from the Controller of Examinations and a dean?

### Takeaway
Only one Registrar from the period has a named, dated source: **Ghulam Ahmad Ashai**, called the first Registrar, **1948–1953**. A 1951 convocation photo caption also shows him as Registrar. **No Registrar for 1953–1989 was found** in any source I opened, for the united university or for the University of Kashmir after 1969. Under the 1969 Act the Registrar, the Controller of Examinations and the Joint Registrar are separate officers. Deans are covered by a separate section.

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia's article on Ashai says Sheikh Mohammad Abdullah "appointed Ghulam Ahmad Ashai as the first Registrar of the University of Kashmir, both for academic and administrative matters, from 1948 to 1953". Before that he "was appointed as the Special Officer assigned with the responsibility of starting Kashmir's first university" and spent two years touring Indian universities.
  - The article names no successor.
  - Its one numbered citation is "Commemorating Ghulam Ahmad Ashai", *Greater Kashmir*, 13 March 2015.
  - It carries a "needs more citations" banner. — [Wikipedia: Ghulam Ahmad Ashai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghulam_Ahmad_Ashai)
- Wikipedia's University of Kashmir article has a photo caption: a 1951 convocation procession of the University of Jammu & Kashmir "led by Ghulam Ahmad Ashai, the registrar". The same caption names "the vice-chancellor, Wazir Janki Nath (chief justice)". The caption is tagged "citation needed". — [Wikipedia: University of Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kashmir)
- Section 9 of the Kashmir and Jammu Universities Act, 1969 lists the officers of each university:
  - Chancellor
  - Pro-Chancellor
  - Vice-Chancellor
  - Pro-Vice-Chancellor, if any
  - Financial Advisor
  - **Registrar**
  - **Controller of Examinations**
  - **Joint Registrar, if any**
  - others the Statutes declare

  Source: [lawgist.in transcription, s.9](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/9)
- Section 18 of the 1969 Act makes the Controller of Examinations a separate whole-time officer, appointed by the University Council on a Selection Committee's recommendation. The Controller handles:
  - setting, printing and custody of question papers
  - conduct of examinations
  - evaluation, tabulation, unfair-means cases, publication of results and rechecking

  The Controller works under the Vice-Chancellor's "immediate direction and control". — [lawgist.in, s.18](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/18)
- The Act's table of contents gives Deans their own section (s.29, "The Deans"). I did not open that section's text. — [lawgist.in, s.17 page navigation](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/17)
- Later context (outside the period): the c.2013 alumni list names "Prof. A.M Mattoo, Registrar" and, as separate posts, "Mr. A.R Koul, Dy. Registrar (T.W.)", "Mr. Zaffar Saif, Dy Registrar, Sub-Office, Jammu" and "Dr. M.I. Nazki, Addl. Controller". This shows the posts kept distinct in practice. — [KU Alumni life-member list](https://Kashmiruniversity.net/events/239.pdf)

### Inferences
- From 1969 the law separates Registrar, Controller of Examinations and dean. A person described as "Registrar and in charge of the education programme" would have to have held two different posts: Registrar plus Dean or Head of Education. Alternatively, "Registrar" may be a loose family label for a different office, such as Deputy or Assistant Registrar, Controller, or Registrar of a college or of the government's Education Department. The sources found cannot tell these apart for any individual.
- Ashai's term ended in 1953, twelve years before the Department of Education was founded in 1965. No opened source links him to Education programmes.
- The convocation date conflicts: a search summary claimed a university PDF dates the first convocation to 24 Sept 1949, against Wikipedia's 1951 caption. I did not open that PDF, so this stays unresolved.

## Q4. What did the Registrar's office do under the statutes?

### Takeaway
Under s.17 of the Kashmir and Jammu Universities Act, 1969, the Registrar was the university's whole-time **administrative and secretarial officer**:
- secretary to the Council, Syndicate, Academic Council, Faculties and Boards of Studies
- custodian of records and the seal
- head of ministerial staff
- collector and disburser of funds
- answerable to the Vice-Chancellor

The Registrar was secretary to the academic bodies that set curricula. The Act does not make the Registrar their academic head.

### Cited Findings
Source for all subsections: [lawgist.in transcription of the Act, s.17](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/17)

- s.17(1): "The Registrar of a University shall be a whole-time officer". Appointed by the University Council on a Selection Committee's recommendation (s.36(1)), on terms set by the Council.
- s.17(2): secretary to the University Council, Syndicate, Academic Council, Faculties, Boards of Studies and other bodies; keeps their minutes.
- s.17(3): controls the university's ministerial, technical and inferior staff, subject to the Vice-Chancellor's sanction and the regulations.
- s.17(4): "the custodian of the records, the common seal" and other property entrusted by the Council or Syndicate.
- s.17(5): collects university money, issues receipts, makes payments and signs cheques, subject to the Statutes.
- s.17(6): issues notices convening meetings of the bodies in s.17(2).
- s.17(7): "be subject to the immediate direction and control of the Vice-Chancellor".
- s.17(8): other powers set by Statutes, Regulations or a university authority.
- s.17(9): "may, with the previous approval of the Vice-Chancellor, delegate any of his powers".

### Inferences
- Under the 1969 Act, a Registrar dealt with the Education programme only administratively: servicing the Faculty and Board of Studies meetings that approved it, issuing notices, keeping minutes and records. Academic responsibility sat with the Faculty, Board of Studies and Academic Council, and with the department's head and dean.
- Wikipedia's description of Ashai as Registrar "for academic and administrative matters" (1948–53) may reflect a broader role before 1969, when the university was small. No 1948 statute was found to confirm it.

### Gaps
- I did not find the Registrar's duties under the 1948 Act and Statutes of the original Jammu and Kashmir University.
- The lawgist.in text is a third-party transcription. The official jklaw.nic.in PDF could not be reached (DNS failure), and the India Code page (indiacode.nic.in/handle/123456789/16526) was not opened. Quotes should be checked against the official text.
- The text of s.29 (Deans) was not opened.

## Q5. Which published lists or jubilee histories name the founding teachers of Education, and which years are missing?

### Takeaway
I found **no published jubilee history, calendar or staff list** online that names the founding (1965) Education teachers, and no year-by-year list of Registrars. The only dated staff evidence is Ashai (Registrar 1948–53) and Madhosh (Dean/Head c.1984–2001, inferred). **Missing years: Registrars 1953–1989; Education heads and faculty 1965–c.1984.**

- The online record is thinnest exactly where the family's claim would sit (Registrar and Education leadership, 1953–1989). It neither confirms nor rules out any particular person. It only shows that the office of Registrar and the leadership of Education were separate in law after 1969, and that before 1965 there was no university Education department to lead.
