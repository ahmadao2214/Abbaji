# Public records confirm the Registrar, not the legend

The University of Kashmir began on **2 November 1948** as the University of Jammu and Kashmir. It was an examining body at Srinagar, set up because Partition had cut the state off from Punjab University at Lahore. It gained teaching departments in **1956** (1958 in one newspaper account), was reorganised in 1965, and was split by ordinance on **5 September 1969** into the universities of Kashmir and Jammu. Set against that timeline, the public record supports a real and substantial life, though not the one the memorial describes. Abdul Aziz Butt (also Bhat or Batt) was born on **26 July 1916** at Kilhotran, Bhalessa, son of Khwaja Sulla Bhat. He entered the state education service in 1941 and worked his way up through headmasterships, the schools inspectorate and college principalships. The university's own 2010 Calendar lists a **"Prof. Abdul Aziz" as Registrar from September 1962 to 1971**. He later ran the Muslim Educational Trust's teachers' college at Sopore (1974–1981) and died in Jammu on **7 August 2016**, twelve days after turning 100. The record does not support the claims that he was founding faculty in 1948, built the university's Education programme, served as a dean, held a doctorate, or was a professor of English or Social Work. The 1948 body did no teaching. The Education department dates itself to 1965 and was headed by Agha Ashraf Ali. Social Work arrived in 2003. The Calendar entry has no surname. His registrarship therefore rests on that entry agreeing with his own interview, a local history and a Kashmir Life obituary. That is a strong identification, but no single document proves it. Three records held by the university would close the gap: the September 1962 appointment order, the 1971 relieving order and the first page of his service book.

## A 1948 examining body became two universities in 1969

The university was a response to the Partition emergency, not a planned teaching foundation. Before 1947 the state's colleges were affiliated to Punjab University at Lahore, which "became inaccessible." Lahore held its last examinations for the state in 1947, and **no university examinations were held in Jammu and Kashmir in 1948** ([Greater Kashmir, "Kashmir University – a flashback," 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)). The university's own 2013 history says that in 1948 "the State Government established an institute for conduct of examinations with its headquarters at Srinagar" ([University of Kashmir, About UoK, archived 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)). The 2010 University Calendar calls it an "affiliating and examining body" ([University Calendar 2010](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/download/UniCal.pdf)). The most detailed narrative found is M. A. Chisti's 2015 reminiscence in Greater Kashmir. It is a reader's piece, not an official history. It says that Ghulam Ahmad Ashai, then Principal of S.P. College, became Special Officer in **July 1948**. His task was to draft the Act, statutes and regulations and to set up the Senate, Syndicate, Academic Council and Faculties. The university was **inaugurated on 2 November 1948**, Ashai became its first Registrar, and the **first examinations followed in March 1949** ([Greater Kashmir, 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)). No source opened gives the founding instrument's title, number or date.

Teaching came later, and the accounts differ on the year. The official 2013 history dates the change to **1956**. That year three postgraduate departments opened, English at Srinagar and Economics and Geology at Jammu, and "thus came into being a full-fledged University, then christerned [sic] the University of Jammu and Kashmir, with Mr. A. A. Fyzee as its first whole-time Vice-Chancellor" ([About UoK, 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)). Greater Kashmir gives **1958** instead. On its account the university took over English from S.P. College, Srinagar, and Economics and Geology from Prince of Wales College, Jammu, then added Urdu, Hindi and Mathematics ([Greater Kashmir, 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)). The 2021 prospectus gives English a 1954 year of establishment ([KU Prospectus 2021](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/Download/Prospectus2021.pdf)). The official 1956 date should lead, with 1958 noted. Wikipedia does not settle the question. Its history paragraph has no inline citation, and its infobox says 1948 while its categories file it under 1956 establishments ([Wikipedia: University of Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kashmir)).

For its first decade the university had no campus. The Registrar's office was in a **government bungalow at Lal Mandi**. In early 1953 it moved to the College of Education to help acquire a proposed site; when that plan fell through in August 1953, the office went back to Lal Mandi. Teaching departments worked from a **rented building at Haft Chinar**, Geology stayed for a time at Gandhi College in Jammu, and convocations moved from Municipal Park to Raj Garh ([Greater Kashmir, 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)). Two sites were tried and abandoned: a hotel-road site, and Hazuri Bagh, where S. Radhakrishnan's foundation stone was later stolen. Dr. Karan Singh then donated land near Hazratbal. The official account calls it "Amar Singh Bagh" and Greater Kashmir calls it "Raja Bagh" ([About UoK, 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx); [Greater Kashmir, 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)). Bakhshi Ghulam Mohammad laid the foundation stone of the Arts Faculty Block on **5 June 1959**. Radhakrishnan, then Vice-President, commissioned it on **1 August 1960** ([About UoK, 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)). The official motto is translated **"From Darkness to Light."** The memorial's phrase **"from the rock" appears in none** of the official pages or newspaper histories opened ([About UoK, 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)).

In 1965 the university was reorganised into Jammu and Kashmir divisions of equal status under a central organisation. G. A. Mukhtar became the first Pro-Vice-Chancellor of the Kashmir division ([About UoK, 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)). The Governor divided the university by ordinance on 5 September 1969. The Kashmir and Jammu Universities Act, 1969 then replaced the ordinance; it **received assent on 30 October 1969** ([Daily Excelsior, 2012](https://www.dailyexcelsior.com/news/archive/from-past-to-present)) and was published in the Gazette on 10 November 1969 ([About UoK, 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx)). Section 3 created the "University of Kashmir" and the "University of Jammu" ([Act s.3](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/3)). Section 52 kept on the employees of the university "constituted under the Jammu and Kashmir University Act, 1965" and let the Chancellor allocate them between the two new universities ([Act s.52](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/52)). A Registrar in office in 1969 would have continued under that provision.

## Civil lists fix his birth at 26 July 1916 and his service from 1941

The strongest evidence about Abdul Aziz Butt comes from **three Jammu and Kashmir government Civil Lists** scanned by the Digital Library of India. The 1947 volume came from the University of Kashmir's own Allama Iqbal Library. Civil List No. 127 is corrected to about October 1952 (the Archive catalogue says 1954). Under "Kashmir Education [Service]," Class III, in a grade that includes "Head Masters (200-15-350)," it records **"19. Abdul Aziz Butt | 26-7-1916 | • | 23-2-1998 | 17-12-2001."** The columns are date of birth, date of entry into government service, and date of promotion to present grade ([Civil List No. 127](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481)). In the Bikrami calendar the state used, entry on 23-2-1998 falls in about **May–June 1941**, and promotion to the grade in about March 1945. Both conversions are the researchers' approximations. The list printed in about January–February 1947 has him as entry 34 in the same cadre ([Civil List 1947](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.449300)). The list for about 1953 still carries him ([Civil List No. 128](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.471671)). These are contemporary government records, compiled while he was serving and independent of the family and of every later book.

The birth date has to be reconciled across four sources. The Civil List gives 26-7-1916. Bashir Bhadarwahi's *The History and Culture of Bhadarwah* (2023) says he "was born in the renowned Bhat family of village Kilhotran Tehsil Bhalesa, to Khwaja Sulla Bhat on 26 July 1916 AD" ([Bhadarwah history, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). Khalid Bashir Ahmad's *Kashmir: A Walk Through History* also gives 26 July 1916 ([Khalid Bashir Ahmad](https://archive.org/details/burzahom-kashmirawalkthroughhistorykhalidbashira-69086)). The family's **January 1916** has no published source. **The memorial should use 26 July 1916.** The day and month come from a contemporary government record, and two books agree with it independently. The family date can be mentioned as family tradition. The father's name, **Khwaja Sulla Bhat**, together with the village of Kilhotran, is the most useful identifier in the whole record. The Kashmir Life obituary also places his birth at Kilhotran, though it puts the village in "Gondu Tehsil near Thatri" rather than Tehsil Bhalesa ([Kashmir Life, 8 Aug 2016](https://kashmirlife.net/day31-witnessed-1947-massacre-of-jammu-muslims-ku-ex-registrar-dies-at-98-114045/)).

His schooling fits the family's memory of Bhalessa and Bhaderwah, but not its account of his college. He was in the first group from Bhalesa admitted to the Bhadarwah school, and a classmate of Ghulam Rasool Azad "since childhood until the 10[th] Class." The Bhadarwah history also calls him "the first M.A. of this place" ([Bhadarwah history, pp. 671, 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). He then "passed his BA from **Prince of Wales College Jammu in 1938**." By his own account he completed an M.A. in Arabic and an LL.B. at Aligarh Muslim University, passed the B.T. teaching degree and cleared Munshi Fazil in the first class ([Bhadarwah history, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). The memorial's "GGM Science College" is that same college under a later name. Greater Kashmir, an opened source, refers to "Prince of Wales College Jammu (now Government Gandhi Memorial)" ([Greater Kashmir, 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)). The exact renaming date of 20 May 1948, however, comes only from search-result text, not a fetched page. **He was not a student at Bhaderwah Degree College.** The Bhadarwah history makes him its **Principal in 1956** ([Bhadarwah history, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). The college's founding year of 1955 was seen only in search-result text, but on any dating the college did not yet exist when he took his BA in 1938.

His first post, as Second Teacher at Kishtwar High School "in the year 1941 AD," matches the Civil List's 1941 entry date ([Bhadarwah history, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah); [Civil List No. 127](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481)). His headmastership is less clear, because two good sources name different schools. Khalid Bashir Ahmad interviewed him in December 2011 and calls him **"Head Master of Hari Singh High School and later Registrar of Jammu & Kashmir University"** ([Khalid Bashir Ahmad](https://archive.org/details/burzahom-kashmirawalkthroughhistorykhalidbashira-69086)). The first research pass summarised that interview as dating the headship from 1946, but the quoted sentence itself gives no year. The 1947 Civil List, read across facing OCR pages, **appears to show him as Head Master of the Government High School, Kishtwar**. His entry 34 lines up with the post "Head Master, [Govern]ment High [School], Kishtwar" on the facing page, and the research rewrite itself rates that match probable, not certain ([Civil List 1947](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.449300)). Both readings should be kept. A Civil List records only government posts, as of the date it was compiled. The research did not establish whether Hari Singh High School was a government school, and a headship at a non-government school would never appear in the list. No opened source makes him a lecturer at Prince of Wales or GGM College. **His early lectureship on record is at Intermediate College, Mirpur**, "where among the students was Krishen Dev Sethi" ([Bhalessa, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). The damaged Bhadarwah text mentions "Lecturer and Headmaster at Mirpur" ([Bhadarwah history, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)).

The 1947 tragedy is supported in outline, but its details need checking before they go on a memorial. The Bhadarwah history's OCR is broken. Its legible fragments say that during the 1947 riots he and his wife **Syeda** were to be taken to Pakistan in a caravan from Jammu. The caravan was attacked "on 5[th] and 6 November 1947 AD" at some distance from Jammu, and "although his daughter fell Prey to the bullets of rioters, the couple" survived ([Bhadarwah history, pp. 774–775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). The Bhalessa book says only "He lost her daughter in turmoil" ([Bhalessa, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). The obituary's headline calls him a witness to the "1947 Massacre Of Jammu Muslims" ([Kashmir Life, 8 Aug 2016](https://kashmirlife.net/day31-witnessed-1947-massacre-of-jammu-muslims-ku-ex-registrar-dies-at-98-114045/)). The first research pass read the Kashmir Life obituary and Khalid Bashir Ahmad's interview-based book. It reported that he was shot in the hand, that the daughter killed was **Masooda, his eldest, about two and a half years old**, and that a second daughter, six months old, was also with them. The name therefore comes from the first pass's opened sources. Two cautions apply. The rewrite's reading of the Bhadarwah passage does not repeat the name. The excerpt kept from the first pass does not quote those lines either. The family should check the wording against the [obituary](https://kashmirlife.net/day31-witnessed-1947-massacre-of-jammu-muslims-ku-ex-registrar-dies-at-98-114045/) and the [book](https://archive.org/details/burzahom-kashmirawalkthroughhistorykhalidbashira-69086) before printing the hand wound or the ages.

After Partition his postings moved to the Kashmir province. The Bhadarwah history makes him Inspector of Schools in Anantnag ([Bhadarwah history, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). In the list of about 1952, the post that lines up with his entry by position is "Assistant Inspector of Schools, Anantnag" ([Civil List No. 127](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509481)). In 1956 he completed a training course in England with Ghulam Rasool Azad and others, and he served as Principal of the Bhadarwah degree college ([Bhadarwah history, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). The Bhalessa book instead makes him "principal of MP School in 1956, 57 and later … principal SP College" ([Bhalessa, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). Before the registrarship, the Bhadarwah history adds that he was **Deputy Director of the Education Department, Kashmir**. Khalid Bashir Ahmad lists him as Secretary of the Jammu and Kashmir Board of School Education, Principal of S.P. College and Inspector of Schools. The Bhalessa book calls the Board post "Chairman" ([Bhadarwah history, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah); [Khalid Bashir Ahmad](https://archive.org/details/burzahom-kashmirawalkthroughhistorykhalidbashira-69086); [Bhalessa, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). "Secretary" comes from the book built on his own interview and "Chairman" from a derivative one, so "Secretary" is the safer word.

## The Calendar lists "Prof. Abdul Aziz" as Registrar from September 1962 to 1971

The only university-issued record of his post is a single line in the Registrar succession list on page xix of the *University Calendar 2010, Vol. I*. Read from the page image, it says: **"4. Prof. Abdul Aziz — Sept1962 – 1971."** It gives **no surname**. The PDF's machine text layer garbles the name as "Abdul Psiz," which is why a text search for "Aziz" misses it. The neighbouring entries frame the tenure. The list begins with G. A. Ashai (1948–Aug 1953) and Kh. Ghulam Mohammad (1953–May 1962). Prof. A. S. Dhar served only from May 1962 to Sept 1962, immediately before him, and Prof. M. A. Chishti served from 1971 to 1975, immediately after ([University Calendar 2010, p. xix](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/download/UniCal.pdf)).

The narrative sources give three different spans. The Bhadarwah history says he "served the chair of Registrar Jammu and Kashmir University from 1962-1969 AD. After the branching out of Jammu University, he continued serving as a Registrar for Kashmir University. He retired from Government services in the year 1974 AD" ([Bhadarwah history, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). Khalid Bashir Ahmad, working from the interview, likewise gives Registrar of the Jammu and Kashmir University from 1962 to 1969 and later of Kashmir University ([Khalid Bashir Ahmad](https://archive.org/details/burzahom-kashmirawalkthroughhistorykhalidbashira-69086)). Kashmir Life's obituary dates his departure to **1966** ([Kashmir Life, 8 Aug 2016](https://kashmirlife.net/day31-witnessed-1947-massacre-of-jammu-muslims-ku-ex-registrar-dies-at-98-114045/)). The Bhalessa book repeats it: he "rose to become registrar of University of Kashmir, a top job he relinquished in 1966" ([Bhalessa, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). A Greater Kashmir column by a university faculty member gives no dates: "Professor Abdul Aziz served as the Registrar & Controller of the Jammu & Kashmir University, he was a great administrator" ([Greater Kashmir, "Lest we forget," 17 Jan 2021](https://www.greaterkashmir.com/opinion/lest-we-forget-6/)).

**The Calendar should govern the dates**, and most of the conflict goes away when the sources are read closely. In the 1962–1969 account, 1969 marks the bifurcation, not his departure, and "continued serving as a Registrar for Kashmir University" points to a later end date. The Calendar supplies that end date: 1971. The start years agree exactly. A Registrar who began in 1962 fits a predecessor who held the post for only four months that year. The 1966 date is the outlier. It also carries an anachronism, since the "University of Kashmir" did not exist under that name until the 1969 Act. One explanation is worth testing, though no source makes it. The 1965 reorganisation created divisional offices, and a 1966 university journal took subscriptions through "The Joint-Registrar, Jammu and Kashmir University, Kashmir Division Srinagar" ([J&K University Review, 1964–66](https://archive.org/metadata/dli.ernet.503903)). A change of title around 1966 could explain the obituary's date. A September 1962 appointment order and a 1971 relieving order would settle it. Nothing found records what he did between 1971 and his 1974 retirement from government service.

The identification of the Calendar's "Prof. Abdul Aziz" with Abdul Aziz Butt is strong, but no single document proves it. The Calendar gives the office and dates without a surname. The surname comes from the books and the obituary. Kashmir Life's headline calls him "KU Ex-Registrar" and gives his Kilhotran birthplace ([Kashmir Life, 8 Aug 2016](https://kashmirlife.net/day31-witnessed-1947-massacre-of-jammu-muslims-ku-ex-registrar-dies-at-98-114045/)). Khalid Bashir Ahmad, writing from his own interview with the man, joins the Hari Singh headmastership to the registrarship. The Bhadarwah history names his father and gives a 1962 start that matches the Calendar. Each source has a weakness, which is why the table below grades them. Two of them rest on his own account. The obituary rests on one informant. The Calendar's name is common in Kashmir, and the university had at least one other "Prof. Abdul Aziz": the mathematician Abdul Aziz-ul-Azeem, Dean of Academic Affairs, who died in June 2021 ([Kashmir Reader, 2021](https://kashmirreader.com/2021/06/30/kus-mathematics-department-condoles-demise-prof-aziz-ul-azeem/)). The first research pass also opened the university's *Sixth Annual Report* (1975–76), which names a "Prof./Shri Abdul Aziz" with no surname. The surviving excerpt does not record his capacity, so it counts neither for nor against ([Sixth Annual Report](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.509430)).

The memorial must keep him separate from his namesakes, and his home village had several. The Bhalessa book lists "Abdul Aziz Batt Former Registrar" separately from an "Abdul Aziz Batt of Kilhotran" and a third man of the same name. It also profiles an Abdul Aziz Batt born in 1934 at Changa, a political figure and former MLC ([Bhalessa, pp. 29–31](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). The Bhadarwah history names an Abdul Aziz Goya Bhat of the Legislative Council. The first research pass found a "Master Abdul Aziz Bhat" in the same book, listed among other men and not as the Registrar ([Bhadarwah history](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). A Plebiscite Front veteran named Abdul Aziz Bhat died at 85 at Poshpora Trehgam ([Kashmir Life](https://kashmirlife.net/abdul-aziz-bhat-plebiscite-front-veteran-and-jknc-worker-passes-away-at-85-374880/)). Spelling cannot tell these men apart. What identifies the Registrar is the 26-7-1916 birth date, Kilhotran, Khwaja Sulla Bhat, the Education Service career and the "former Registrar" label.

The office itself also limits what the memorial can claim. Under section 17 of the 1969 Act, the Registrar was a whole-time administrative officer. He served as secretary to the Council, Syndicate, Academic Council, Faculties and Boards of Studies, kept the records and seal, and answered to the Vice-Chancellor ([Act s.17](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/17)). The same Act made the Controller of Examinations a separate officer ([Act s.18](https://lawgist.in/states/jammu-and-kashmir/kashmir-and-jammu-universities-act-1969/18)). The Greater Kashmir column's "Registrar & Controller" fits the pre-1969 university, whose statutes on the point were not found. A Registrar took the minutes of the bodies that approved curricula, but he did not lead them.

## The university's Education department was not his to build

**He was not founding faculty in 1948.** The 1948 body examined students and did no teaching, its teaching departments began in 1956 or 1958, and all three Civil Lists keep him in the state school cadre from 1947 to about 1953 ([About UoK, 2013](https://web.archive.org/web/20130501010818/http://kashmiruniversity.net/aboutuok.aspx); [Civil List 1947](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.449300); [Civil List No. 128](https://archive.org/details/dli.ernet.471671)). The university's own Department of Education says it "was established in the year **1965**," beginning with a one-year M.Ed. It added a two-year M.A. in 1979 and M.Phil. and Ph.D. research in the early 1980s, and its history names no founders ([KU Department of Education](https://education.uok.edu.in/Main/AboutUs.aspx)). A period source names its head. The university's own *Review* for 1964–66 reports that "the Post-graduate Departments of Education and History … have started functioning," and its contributor list names **"Dr. Ashraf Agha, Head of Post-graduate Department of Education"** ([J&K University Review, 1964–66](https://archive.org/metadata/dli.ernet.503903)). Later tributes say the same. Kashmir Life, in a 2020 obituary written by a relative, says Agha Ashraf Ali's efforts "led to the setting up of Department of Education" and that he began heading it in **1968** ([Kashmir Life, 26 Aug 2020](https://kashmirlife.net/agha-ashraf-ali-1922-2020-issue-21-vol-12-243771/)). A 2024 Kashmir Times piece credits him with establishing it in 1968 ([Kashmir Times, 2024](https://kashmirtimes.com/opinion/comment-articles/wit-wisdom-and-oratory-kashmirs-beloved-and-eloquent-scholar)). The Greater Kashmir column calls him "founder Dean & Head" and mentions furniture he procured for the Dean's chamber in 1967 ([Greater Kashmir, 2021](https://www.greaterkashmir.com/opinion/lest-we-forget-6/)). "Dr. Ashraf Agha" and "Agha Ashraf Ali" are almost certainly the same man, though that is an inference. Either way, every source puts someone other than Abdul Aziz at the head of the department. The same column names Abdul Aziz only as Registrar.

Before 1965, teacher training in the state ran through government institutions. Until 1947 there were two training schools, at Srinagar and Jammu, with places for 200 teachers. A Government of India review of 1947–61 then records "two post-graduate training colleges for teachers," one in each city ([Kashmir Life, "Schooling In 1947," 2022](https://kashmirlife.net/schooling-in-1947-vol-14-issue-22-299084/)). A College of Education was operating in Srinagar by early 1953, when the Registrar's office briefly moved into it ([Greater Kashmir, 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20210524084223/https://www.greaterkashmir.com/news/opinion/kashmir-university-a-flashback/)). Agha Ashraf Ali was officiating principal of the Teachers College of Education from 1954 to 1960 ([Kashmir Life, 2020](https://kashmirlife.net/agha-ashraf-ali-1922-2020-issue-21-vol-12-243771/)). The research did not establish whether a university Faculty of Education examined teacher-training candidates before 1965. The 1950s statute book was examined only for its list of offices, and the education notes leave the question open. When the university printed its first Calendar in 1972, the Faculty of Education offered a one-year M.Ed. ([University Calendar 2010](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/download/UniCal.pdf)).

**His documented education work is substantial, but it lies outside the university's Education department.** He spent three decades in the state's education service as teacher, headmaster, Inspector of Schools, Deputy Director of Education in Kashmir and college principal, and served with the Board of School Education. He took a training course in England in 1956 ([Bhadarwah history, pp. 774–775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). After retiring in 1974 he became Principal of the Muslim Educational Trust's College of Education at Sopore, a teachers' college, until 1981 ([Khalid Bashir Ahmad](https://archive.org/details/burzahom-kashmirawalkthroughhistorykhalidbashira-69086); [Bhalessa, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). The Bhadarwah history gives that post as "the next five years." It also says that in 1986 he "laid the foundation of College of Education in Model Institute of Education Jammu" and became adviser to its director ([Bhadarwah history, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)). If "responsible for the education programme" means anything documented, it most plausibly means this school-system and teacher-training career. That is an inference; no source uses the phrase. None of these posts should be turned into a deanship or a doctorate, and neither should the registrarship or the 1956 course.

The titles also need correcting. **No source found calls him "Dr."** His degree lists show an M.A., LL.B., B.T. and Munshi Fazil, and the Bhalessa book adds "H.P., E.E.T&P (London)." No list includes a doctorate ([Bhadarwah history, p. 774](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah); [Bhalessa, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). The Calendar and the books call him "Prof.," but **no source attaches a subject or department**, and none makes him a professor of English, Education or Social Work. English existed in his working years, with a year of establishment given as 1954, but no source places him on its faculty. **Social Work was founded in 2003**, twenty-nine years after he retired ([KU Prospectus 2021](https://www.kashmiruniversity.net/Download/Prospectus2021.pdf)). The memorial's Oxford and Warwick guest lectures in the 1980s also found no support. The only British connection on record is the 1956 training course in England ([Bhadarwah history, p. 775](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)).

## He died in Jammu on 7 August 2016, not in April 2017

Two sources the first research pass opened fix the death. Muhammad Raafi's Kashmir Life obituary of 8 August 2016, "KU Ex-Registrar Dies At 98," says "he breathed his last in Jammu on Sunday." It adds that he "lived in Gogjibagh area in Srinagar" and was "survived by three sons and three daughters," naming Javid Ahmad, "first Muslim commercial pilot of Kashmir," and Altaf Ahmad, a retired executive engineer. Its information came from one informant, Jalal Ud Din Shah ([Kashmir Life, 8 Aug 2016](https://kashmirlife.net/day31-witnessed-1947-massacre-of-jammu-muslims-ku-ex-registrar-dies-at-98-114045/)). Khalid Bashir Ahmad writes that he "passed away on **7 August 2016**, twelve days after he had completed 100 years of his life" ([Khalid Bashir Ahmad](https://archive.org/details/burzahom-kashmirawalkthroughhistorykhalidbashira-69086)). The two agree, because 7 August 2016 was a Sunday. The book's arithmetic also matches a 26 July 1916 birth exactly. The headline's "98" is the obituary's error, and **"aged 100" is the supported figure.** He was alive and active late in life: in February 2006 he took part in a Jammu felicitation for Ghulam Nabi Azad ([Bhadarwah history](https://archive.org/details/the-history-and-culture-of-bhadarwah)), and in December 2011 he gave Khalid Bashir Ahmad the interview ([Khalid Bashir Ahmad](https://archive.org/details/burzahom-kashmirawalkthroughhistorykhalidbashira-69086)).

The rival date comes from a KNS wire notice posted on **7 April 2017**: "prominent educationist **Master** Abdul Aziz Bhat is no more." It gives no age, locality, date of death or posts held ([KNS, 7 Apr 2017](https://www.knskashmir.com/Eminent-educationist-Abdul-Aziz-Bhat-passes-away-15731)). The Bhalessa book copies its opening sentence word for word but changes "Master" to "Professor," directly under the Registrar's biography ([Bhalessa, p. 32](https://archive.org/details/b-h-a-l-e-s-s-a-by-sadaket-malik)). The same book also copies the 2016 obituary, so it has merged two different death notices. **The KNS notice does not identify the Registrar**, and the Bhadarwah history lists a separate "Master Abdul Aziz Bhat" among other men. The April 2017 date should not replace August 2016. The research rewrite treated the death as unresolved because its web searches missed the Kashmir Life page. A missed search is not evidence that the obituary does not exist, and the first pass had already opened it.

## Conclusion

Almost every unsupported line on the memorial sits next to a documented fact that it has inflated. He was Principal of the Bhaderwah college, so he became its student. A 1956 course in England became Oxford and Warwick lectures. A registrarship became a deanship, a title of "Prof." became professorships in named subjects, and decades of teacher training became the founding of the university's Education department. A tenure that ended in 1971 drifted into "the 1970s." The correction is therefore mostly restoration: put back the real post the legend grew from. What survives is a rarer story than the legend. A boy from a Bhalessa village became, by the Bhadarwah history's account, the area's first M.A. He survived the November 1947 caravan killings, rose through the state's school system, and, by the university's own list, served as Registrar through its 1965 reorganisation and its 1969 split. The most reliable facts are also the plainest ones, the dates on a 1952 Civil List. They outweigh later memory.

One gap stands between that story and a university-acknowledged profile: the surname missing from the Calendar's line. Three documents, all likely in the Registrar's establishment files at Hazratbal, would close it. The first is the **September 1962 appointment order**, which would give his full name and the exact post. The second is the **1971 relieving or handover order**, which would fix the end date and show whether he was Registrar or Joint Registrar in each phase. The third is the **first page of his service book**, which, matched to Khwaja Sulla Bhat or Kilhotran and to the 26-7-1916 birth date, would tie "Prof. Abdul Aziz" to the man in the Civil Lists. The family can request them through the university's RTI Information Cell or by a legal heir's letter enclosing the death certificate. If they exist, the university could add his surname to its succession list, or publish a Former Registrars page alongside its existing Former Vice-Chancellors page. A Chancellor's order of 1969–70 naming the new university's first Registrar under the Act would also settle whether he held that distinction, which no source yet states.
